How do you uncork something you're not allowed to show?

A brand, a website, a full set of event materials and a LinkedIn presence for Stavanger's new showcase and networking pavilion, built in about ten weeks from a name and a legal problem.

The challenge

A new pavilion, a bold name and very little time

Every August, ONS brings around [70,000, confirm on ons.no] energy professionals to Stavanger. The exhibition halls are busy, loud and overwhelming. Many of the conversations that actually move business forward happen later, downtown.

For 2026, Company Outing brought together Innovation Norway, Export Finance Norway and a group of technology and property partners to build one shared pavilion for exactly those conversations: a place to meet, showcase and do business. The partners voted on a name, and the pavilion became UnCorked.

That gave us our first problem. "Uncorked" means popping a bottle, and in Norway, alcohol advertising is strictly limited. The brief was clear: no bottles, no glasses, no drinks. What gets uncorked here is innovation, connections and new technology.

The client summed up the feel in two words: corporate edgy. Professional enough for government partners, fun enough to stand out from the grey of a typical conference, and as welcoming as walking into a good hotel lobby.

  • An identity that suggests release and energy, without showing what is being opened
  • A "corporate edgy" look: fun and energetic, but still professional
  • A logo that stands alone, and also sits alongside the ONS+ mark
  • A website the client's own team could update as the programme kept changing
  • Everything ready for 24 August, with content still being decided along the way

1
Brand identity

Bubbling, not bottled

Three days after kickoff, we presented a wide range of logo directions, from bold block wordmarks to playful scripts and colour combinations. The client chose a clean, confident wordmark with sparkles rising out of it. It suggests energy being released without ever showing what is being opened.

The client also had a bigger idea: rockets, wind turbines and technology bursting out of the logo, because that is exactly what UnCorked releases. We advised against putting them in the logo itself. A logo packed with icons becomes unreadable on a badge or a phone screen. Instead, we turned the idea into a separate graphic system of sparkles, bursts and patterns that carries the story across the website, social posts and print. The client got everything he asked for, in a form that works at every size.

We tested four colour schemes directly on the website layout. The client chose deep purple and electric lime: energetic, high in contrast and still professional.

2
Website

A one-page hub the client could run

uncorked.no was designed as a single, scrollable page that answers the questions visitors actually have: what is this, what is on, how do I join, and where is it.

Built for the client to run. When the client approved the project, he had one condition: his team had to manage the content themselves, because the programme would keep changing until the final week. We built the site in WordPress with modular sections the team could edit, hide and reorder without calling a developer. The programme ran as an embedded Luma calendar, which already handled registrations and waiting lists.

Simple where it counts. Partner enquiries became a single "Request a spot" button that opens a ready-addressed email for showcase, speaking, sponsorship or partnership requests, with no form to build or maintain. The news section was built at launch but kept hidden until there was something worth publishing.

Getting the word out early. Promotion didn't wait for the site. In the first week, we delivered event cover images for LinkedIn and Facebook, so the client could start promoting while the programme was still being written.

Event and print design

From the street to the last drink voucher

With three weeks to go, the project moved from screens to the street. We set up a dedicated in-house design team for the final stretch, because events always bring last-minute changes and there was no time to wait for them.

Our rule for everything printed was simple: if it doesn't look real in print, it doesn't go out. Every piece used the same system, from large-format banners to the smallest handout.

Partner and host badges, with a QR code straight to the programme.

Building awareness before the doors opened
